# Where university sites keep the numbers

- **Program page** (the `Website Link`): credit hours, duration, delivery format, tracks, experiential
  learning, sometimes cost and deadlines. Follow its "Admissions", "Tuition & Financial Aid",
  "Class Profile", "Curriculum", "Careers / Outcomes" tabs first.
- **Admissions page**: deadlines (often a table per term), test requirements (GMAT/GRE waived?),
  international requirements (3-year degree, English tests), deposit to hold a seat.
- **Tuition pages** are often off the program site: bursar / student accounts / "tuition and fees"
  tables per term, or a graduate-school-wide rate page. Online programs often have one flat per-credit rate
  with no in-state/out-of-state difference; then the same rate applies to all three residency columns
  (say so only if the page says it applies to all students, e.g. "regardless of residency").
- **Class profile / "Our Students"**: GMAT/GRE averages, GPA, work experience, age, class size,
  international %, women %. Check the heading's year.
- **Employment / outcomes report**: placement %, salary, bonus. Usually full-time programs only; online
  programs often publish none (then "not found").
- **Diversity / student life / clubs**: affinity groups (e.g. Women in Business, Black MBA association,
  Out for Business, veterans club) -- the online program's students must have access.
- **Career services**: coaching, one-on-one advising available to online students.
- Online program sites are often run by a partner (e.g. `online.<school>.edu`, `<school>online.com`);
  those pages count as the school's own site.
- PDFs (viewbooks, tuition schedules) are not read; look for the HTML page that states the same fact.
